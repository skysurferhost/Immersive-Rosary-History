## Historical recommendations

- Origins: retain gradual medieval development, not a complete modern Rosary in the first centuries. The Catholic Encyclopedia’s Rosary history supports this distinction.
- Hail Mary: Luke 1:28 and 1:42 underpin the biblical greetings; distinguish these from the later complete prayer. Marialis Cultus §§44–49 supports the scriptural and Christ-centered account.
- St Dominic: preserve explicit devotional-tradition wording; do not present a 1214 apparition or complete modern form as documented thirteenth-century fact. The Dominican Confraternity page identifies the apparition account as tradition.
- Marian Psalter: prefer early fifteenth century to c. 1409 as a specific invention date. The Dominic of Prussia biography identifies 1409 as monastery entry. Christ-life clauses are supported; no universal physical bead topology follows.
- Confraternity: the Dominican Order supports Cologne, 1475, Jacob Sprenger and Alan de la Roche’s fifteenth-century promotion.
- Pius V: the current English Dominican page is relevant and explicitly quotes Consueverunt and identifies 1569. Align its source label or also link Marialis Cultus §§42,49, which the label names.
- Lepanto: Leo XIII’s Supremi Apostolatus Officio §4 supports Catholic attribution to Mary and feast commemoration. Use §4 for precision; retain separate support for exact battle date/geography. Move artistic-generation distinctions to discreet credits.
- Leo XIII: 1 September 1883 and §8’s October observances are supported.
- Fátima: official shrine narratives support the 1917 reported apparitions and daily Rosary request. Catechism §67 distinguishes private revelation from definitive Revelation in Christ. A destination’s inclusion in the 2021 schedule does not authenticate every associated apparition claim.
- Paul VI: 2 February 1974; Marialis Cultus §§42–55 supports a scriptural, contemplative, Christ-centered Rosary.
- John Paul II: Rosarium Virginis Mariae, 16 October 2002, §§19–21 supports all five Luminous Mysteries: Baptism; Cana with Mary and Jesus’ first sign; Kingdom/conversion/forgiveness; Transfiguration; Eucharist. Gospel references: Matthew 3:13–17, John 2:1–11, Mark 1:14–15, Matthew 17:1–8, Matthew 26:26–29.

## Medieval artifact evidence

[London Museum object 5079](https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/v/object-31669/beads-rosary-beads/) is catalogued to the fourteenth–fifteenth century with **wood and cotton** as materials. It supports representative wooden beads on a continuous cotton cord. The description recognizes varying counting sequences. Do not claim the artwork is an exact replica, that medieval strings universally held 150 beads, or that modern 59-bead topology was already standard.

The [Met paternoster pendant](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/459203), circa 1440–50, corroborates late-medieval bead strings. Its record includes a nineteenth/twentieth-century component; do not call the full assemblage unaltered medieval.

The [Dynamic Catholic overview](https://www.dynamiccatholic.com/rosary/history-of-the-rosary.html) is useful devotional background, not controlling chronology. Its unqualified Dominic apparition account, early five-decade claim and attribution of the standardized three cycles to Dominic of Prussia should not override qualified Dominican tradition, Vatican teaching or museum evidence.
